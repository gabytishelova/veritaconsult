Fractional leadership or a full-time hire

16 June 2026 · 4 min read

When a scaling operator needs senior product, delivery or commercial leadership, there are two obvious routes. Hire someone full time, or bring in a senior person for part of the week for a limited period. Each suits a different problem.

A full-time hire fits when the role is permanent and the work is daily. If the person needs to run a team of ten, hold one-to-ones, hire, coach, set the culture and be reachable all day, you need someone whose job is only that. The cost is higher and so is the commitment. The search takes months, and a senior person in iGaming often needs a notice period of three to six months before starting.

Interim or fractional leadership fits when the problem has a shape and an end. A platform migration. A market entry. A product team that lost its lead and needs steering for two quarters. A retention function that needs rebuilding before someone permanent takes it over. The person arrives with the experience, starts in weeks rather than months, and leaves behind a team, a plan and a clear brief for the successor.

Say an operator plans to enter a second regulated market in nine months. It has a strong head of product for the existing business and nobody who has run a launch. A full-time hire would arrive in four months, leaving five to deliver. A senior interim lead could start in three weeks, set the plan, brief the team and hand over once the market is live.

The decision depends on four questions.

What outcome do you need in the next ninety days? If you can write it in one sentence, interim support can probably deliver it. If the answer is "run the department", you need a hire.

How much of the work is judgement and how much is management? Judgement can be bought by the day. Management of people needs presence.

Is the role going to exist in two years? If the answer is unclear, an interim arrangement lets you find out before you commit.

Do you know what good looks like? Operators that have never hired for the role often write a vague brief and pick the wrong person. A fractional leader can help define the full-time role and sit on the interview panel.

The risks on each side are different.

A full-time hire may turn out to be the wrong fit, and unwinding it costs time, money and morale. References from a similar business help, as do clear goals for the first 90 days.

A fractional leader splits attention. If they work with several clients, you need to know how much time you get and when. Insist on a fixed cadence, stated hours and a named deputy for urgent issues. Decision rights have to be clear, because a senior person with no authority to decide becomes an expensive commentator. And an arrangement with no end date tends to drift into a permanent one at part-time efficiency.

A 90-day outcome might read like this. The launch plan is agreed and funded. Owners are named for every workstream. The first two milestones are delivered. A hiring brief for a permanent lead is approved. If the interim has delivered those four items, the business can move on whether or not the engagement continues. If the outcomes cannot be written down this plainly, the problem is probably not ready for outside help.

An interim engagement works best with a tight set-up. Define the scope as outcomes with dates, not as hours. Agree who the person reports to and which decisions they can take alone. Set a review at 30 and 60 days, with a clear option to stop. Require a written handover from the start, so knowledge sits with your team and not only with the interim. Check references with former clients and ask what happened after the person left.

Think about the cost on the same basis as any hire. A day rate looks high next to a salary until you add recruitment fees, benefits, equity, the time lost to a vacancy and the cost of a poor hire. It looks high again if the engagement has no clear end. Neither comparison is useful without a defined outcome.

We offer embedded advisory support of this kind, so we have a view, and we also tell clients when a permanent hire is the better answer. If the work is a continuing line role, hire for it.
